## Overview

**Negocios Uno** is a platform that helps small and medium businesses manage their day-to-day operations. The client had a working product but no web presence — potential users were landing on nothing after hearing about it through social channels.

This was the first project where a real person paid me to build something, reviewed the work, and needed it deployed and working by a specific date.

---

## Role
**Freelance Frontend Developer** — requirements gathering, design implementation, development, and delivery.

---

## The Problem

The client had a product and users but no web presence. The ask was concrete: a professional landing page that communicates the platform's value to business owners who aren't necessarily technical, with a clear CTA, and live within two weeks.

---

## Solution

A static marketing site with a sharp content hierarchy: lead with the value proposition, follow with features, close with social proof and contact.

### Tech Stack

| Layer | Technology |
|-------|-----------|
| Framework | Astro |
| Styling | TailwindCSS |
| Deployment | Vercel |

**Why Astro for a landing page?**
The site is entirely marketing content — no login, no dynamic data, no client-side interactivity beyond the contact CTA. Astro's static output ships as raw HTML with zero JavaScript overhead. For a page where load time directly affects conversion, this was the obvious choice.

**Constraint-driven decision making:**
Two weeks, solo, non-technical client with feedback via screenshots and written notes. Astro's file-based routing and component model made the project fast to reason about and easy to iterate on based on client input.

---

## What I Built

- Hero section with primary value proposition and CTA
- Features breakdown for SME decision-makers
- Testimonials section
- Contact section with clear next steps
- Mobile-first responsive layout across all breakpoints
- SEO metadata: title, description, Open Graph tags

---

## What Working With a Real Client Taught Me

No tutorial covers what a real client relationship surfaces:

- **Requirements change mid-build**: having a component-based architecture made pivoting on section order and content painless — change the component, done
- **Non-technical feedback is specific but described visually**: "make it feel more serious" translates to adjusting font weight, tightening spacing, and pulling back on color saturation — not redesigning the layout
- **Delivery means deployed and working**: not running locally, not staged behind a password — live, on the actual domain, working on mobile

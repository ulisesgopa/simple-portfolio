## Overview

**MiPOS** is a point-of-sale SaaS for small and medium businesses in Colombia: sell, invoice and control the business from one place, valid before the DIAN (Colombia's tax authority).

It started as a POS with an admin dashboard. In 2026 it became a multi-tenant product with subscriptions, branches, installable desktop registers that work without internet, and a platform panel to run every client business. In October 2026 it went live with its **first paying client in Colombia**, issuing real electronic invoices in production.

## The Problem

Colombian SMEs (grocery stores, butchers, restaurants) are legally required to issue electronic invoices, but:

- Affordable POS systems either skip the DIAN integration or lock it behind expensive plans
- Many shops have unstable internet. A cloud-only POS that stops selling when the connection drops is not an option
- Invoicing mistakes aren't cosmetic. A duplicated sale can mean a duplicated **legal invoice**

## What's new in 2026

### From MVP to production SaaS

- **Zero-friction onboarding**: sign up with email and password, try it in sandbox, configure taxes later
- **Subscriptions with hard-lock**: an expired plan blocks every write endpoint (reading stays open, so the business never loses access to its data)
- **Platform superadmin**: businesses operating, blocked or about to expire; per-business e-invoicing checklist, numbering ranges, monthly signed/rejected documents; explicit confirmation before switching a business from **sandbox to production**
- **Roles**: admin, cashier and a read-only **accountant** role with a reports screen and accounting PDF export

### Point of sale for real shops

- **Restaurant tables** (Horeca): open tabs, progressive orders, non-fiscal pre-bill and close-to-sale
- **Credit and accounts receivable** with installment payments
- **Sales on hold**: save the cart and resume it from any register; drafts can be printed
- **VAT only applies when the sale carries an electronic invoice**
- **Direct USB thermal printing** from the browser (ESC/POS via WebUSB), 80mm tickets with the invoice QR, X/Z cash reports
- **Branches and register codes** in sale numbers and cash closings

### Desktop registers that work offline

An **Electron app** that bundles an embedded PostgreSQL, the backend and the POS UI on `127.0.0.1`. It sells without internet and syncs to the cloud a few seconds after each sale, cash opening or closing. Rejected records are retried, and each register shows its version, connection and last sync.

- Register linking with superadmin approval and an **Ed25519-signed license**
- Installation secrets encrypted with the OS keychain
- System printer without dialog and **serial-port scales** for products sold by weight
- Builds for macOS (DMG) and Windows (NSIS)

## Pre-go-live audit

Before the first client went live, I audited sales, tables, invoicing, subscriptions and the superadmin against a disposable PostgreSQL database. Some of the critical fixes:

| Issue | Fix | Verified with |
|-------|-----|---------------|
| Duplicate sales under concurrency (double stock, double cash, double DIAN invoice) | Idempotency key `create` + catch `P2002` inside the transaction → 409 `SALE_DUPLICATE` | 5 simultaneous requests → 1 sale |
| Retries generated a new idempotency key on each attempt | Key persists while the cart is unchanged | Typecheck |
| Factus rejection left the cart full, so the cashier charged again | Sale is saved and returns 201 with `invoiceStatus: rejected` | Failed invoice → 201 + error stored |
| Double payment of a restaurant tab | Atomic `OPEN → PAID` at the start of the transaction | 4 simultaneous payments → 1 sale |
| Race condition on credit payments | `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the sale | 5 simultaneous 60% payments → 1 accepted |
| Cashier could sell at any price (even $0 with an invoice) | Only admins override prices | Cashier → 403 |
| Refresh tokens without rotation | Rotation on every refresh + reuse detection revokes all sessions | Rotation, multi-tab and stolen-token cases |
| Legal invoice retries in production with a new reference | Retry queries Factus by reference first; no new references in production | Typecheck |

The backend went from **12 TypeScript errors to 0**, and every Factus call got a timeout so a slow provider can't freeze a sale.

## Final Thoughts

Getting a POS to production isn't about the happy path. It's about what happens when two cashiers press "charge" at the same time, when the tax provider is slow, or when the internet goes down mid-sale. MiPOS went from a working demo to software a real business trusts with its legal invoices, and that gap was closed with concurrency tests, audits and paying attention to how the shop actually operates.

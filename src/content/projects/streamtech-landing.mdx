## Overview

**Stream Tech** provides automation solutions for livestreams, TV channels, and virtual events. For a startup in an early growth stage, the marketing site is the first — and often only — impression with potential clients and investors.

I was the person who wrote the code and the one responsible for the business outcome at the same time. That dual perspective shaped every technical decision.

---

## Role

**CEO & Frontend Developer** — sole responsible for design direction, code, deployment, and production maintenance.

---

## The Problem

A new company in the media technology space has two audiences reading the same site with completely different questions:

- **Potential clients** want to understand what the service does and whether to contact us
- **Startup program evaluators** (Ruta de Emprendimiento 2024) want to see a credible, professional digital presence

A slow or poorly designed site fails both.

---

## Technical Decisions

**Why Astro over Next.js?**

Stream Tech's landing is static content — services, case studies, contact info. There is no user authentication, no real-time data, no client-side state. Next.js would send React's runtime JavaScript to every visitor for a page that never uses it.

Astro generates pure HTML at build time with zero JavaScript by default. The result: faster Time to First Byte, better Core Web Vitals scores, and simpler deployment to a CDN — which matters when the site is part of a business pitch.

**Why TailwindCSS?**

A startup landing goes through multiple rounds of copy and layout feedback from non-technical stakeholders. Tailwind's utility classes make visual iterations fast without touching a separate stylesheet. Changes happen in the component file directly.

## What I'd Improve Now

With what I know today, I'd add:

## Final Thoughts

Building a site for your own company is a different experience than client work. Every performance tradeoff has a direct business consequence. That context reinforced why framework choice matters — not as a preference, but as a product decision.

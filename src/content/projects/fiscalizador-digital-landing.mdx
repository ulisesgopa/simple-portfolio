## Overview

**Fiscalizador Digital** is a mobile application for traffic enforcement officers — it replaces physical ticket books with a digital workflow that includes camera-based evidence capture, GPS location, automatic license plate recognition, and real-time notifications to the vehicle owner.

My role was building the public landing page that communicates this product to its two target audiences: government procurement teams and police/transit agencies.

---

## Role

**Freelance Frontend Developer** (Tecnología Urbana) — full implementation of the marketing site.

---

## The Problem

Govtech products face a unique trust problem: the buyer is a government agency with procurement processes, legal requirements, and multiple approvers. The landing page is often reviewed by officials who are not developers but who need to understand what the system does, how it integrates, and whether it's credible enough to proceed to a demo.

This is fundamentally different from a B2C landing page. The conversion goal isn't a button click — it's a meeting.

---

## Technical Decisions

**Why Next.js?**

Government procurement evaluators often share links and review sites on institutional networks. SEO and link preview quality (Open Graph) affect whether a site looks legitimate in an email forward.

Next.js with server-side rendering meant every page URL produced a fully-rendered HTML response — better for crawlers, better for link previews, better for institutional credibility. A Vite SPA would have sent an empty shell on first load.

**Content hierarchy driven by the buyer journey:**

The sections weren't built in arbitrary order — they follow the questions a procurement evaluator asks in sequence:
1. What does this do? (Hero)
2. What specific problem does it solve for officers? (Features)
3. How does the workflow actually look? (User flow)
4. What integrations does it have? (Technical specs)
5. How do we get a demo? (CTA)

## What This Project Taught Me

Govtech has a completely different conversion funnel from typical SaaS or consumer products. "Move fast and ship" is the wrong mindset — the content needs to be precise, authoritative, and answer objections before they're raised. That changed how I approached information architecture.
